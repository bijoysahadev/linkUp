1. Methods for Obtaining New Products

  • Acquisition: Buying an entire company, a patent, or a license to manufacture someone else's product.
  • New-Product Development (NPD): Developing original products, improvements, modifications, and new brands via the company's internal research and development (R&D).

2. The 8 Stages of the NPD Process

[1. Idea Generation] ➔ [2. Idea Screening] ➔ [3. Concept Dev & Testing] ➔ [4. Marketing Strategy Dev] │ [8. Commercialization]  [7. Test Marketing]  [6. Product Development]  [5. Business Analysis] ↵
  1. Idea Generation: Systematic search for new-product ideas.
    • Internal Sources: Formal R&D, executive team, staff, and internal intrapreneurial programs.
    • External Sources: Customers, competitors, distributors, suppliers, outside design consultancies.
    • Crowdsourcing: Inviting broad communities (customers, employees, independent scientists, public) into the innovation process.
  2. Idea Screening: Spotting good ideas and discarding bad ones early.
    • R-W-W Framework: Is it Real? (Is there a real demand?) | Can we Win? (Competitive advantage?) | Is it Worth doing? (Sufficient ROI?)
  3. Concept Development and Testing:
    • Product Idea: An idea for a possible product the company can envision itself offering.
    • Product Concept: Detailed version of the idea expressed in meaningful consumer terms.
    • Product Image: The way consumers perceive an actual or potential product.
    • Concept Testing: Testing concepts with target consumer groups.
  4. Marketing Strategy Development: Initial strategy statement divided into 3 parts:
    1. Target market description, planned value proposition, and sales/profit goals for year 1.
    2. Planned price, distribution strategy, and marketing budget.
    3. Long-term sales targets, profit goals, and marketing mix strategy over time.
  5. Business Analysis: Reviewing sales, cost, and profit projections to check alignment with organizational goals.
  6. Product Development: R&D/engineering creates physical prototypes to test technical and functional feasibility (requires a large capital jump).
  7. Test Marketing: Testing product and marketing strategy in realistic market settings.
    • 3 Types: Standard, Controlled, and Simulated test markets.
    • Simulated Markets: Faster, cheaper, keeps ideas hidden from competitors, but less accurate due to artificial settings.
    • Skip test marketing when: Launching simple line extensions, copying competitor products, low investments, or high executive confidence.
  8. Commercialization: Full-scale market rollout. Requires timing decisions (when to launch) and spatial rollout plans (where to launch).

3. NPD Organizational Approaches

  • Customer-Centered: Focuses on finding new ways to solve customer problems and create satisfying customer experiences.
  • Sequential: Departments work individually step-by-step; offers control for risky projects, but can be slow.
  • Team-Based: Cross-functional teams work concurrently/overlapping to save time and increase speed to market.
  • Systematic: Uses an idea management system to systematically collect, review, and evaluate ideas.

4. Product Life-Cycle (PLC) Curves & Stage Dynamics

PLC Stage Sales Trend Profit Dynamic Main Marketing Objective Communication Focus
Introduction Slow growth Negative / Low Encourage product trial; inform buyers Informative advertising about usage and benefits
Growth Rapidly rising Peaks (mid/late stage) Build brand preference; maximize market share Shift toward sales promotions; brand building
Maturity Peak (then plateaus) Declining (price cuts) Defend market share; find new segments Reminder advertising
Decline Falling continuous Eroding to near zero Harvest, maintain limited run, or drop product Minimal to zero promotion spending

5. Special PLC Variants

  • Style: A basic and distinctive mode of expression. Shows a wave-like curve cycling through periods of renewed popularity across generations.
  • Fashion: A currently accepted style in a given field. Follows a smooth bell curve (grows slowly, stays popular, declines slowly).
  • Fad: Temporary period of unusually high sales driven by immediate popularity. Shows a sharp spike up and an equally fast drop.
